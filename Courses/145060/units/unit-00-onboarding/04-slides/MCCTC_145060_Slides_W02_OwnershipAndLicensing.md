## Slide 4: Licenses are not contested
- MIT: use it, keep the notice
- Apache 2.0: similar, plus patent terms
- GPL: use it, and publish your own source
- No license at all is not permission
Speaker notes: This part is settled and checkable, so learn it properly. These three licenses ask genuinely different things of you. The last bullet is the one people get wrong. Code posted publicly with no license attached is not free to take. Public means visible. It does not mean permitted. You are going to read all three of these today and write one sentence each on what they require.

## Slide 5: Where to actually find a license
```
On any GitHub repository, top level folder:

  LICENSE          most common
  LICENSE.md       same thing
  COPYING          older projects, usually GPL

No such file?  Then no permission was granted.
```
Speaker notes: This is where you look, and it takes about eight seconds. The file sits at the top level of the repository next to the README. If you open a project and there is no license file at all, that is not a shortcut. That is the author not having granted anyone permission, and the safe reading is that you may not use it.

## Slide 6: What a log entry actually looks like
```
Model used:
What I asked:
What came back, in summary:
What I kept:
What I changed, and why:
How I verified it:
```
Speaker notes: This is the template and the last two lines are the graded ones. The first four are record keeping and anybody can fill them in. What I changed and why is where your thinking shows. How I verified it is where I find out whether you checked. It looked right is not verification and it will not earn the points. Today you write your first complete one.

## Slide 7: The question, and both sides are serious
- Does the prompt writer own the output
- Yes: you chose, judged, revised, integrated
- No: it was assembled from other people's work
- Pick a side and argue the other one honestly
Speaker notes: Here is the question you are writing about today. Take both sides seriously, because both are serious. On one side, you chose the problem, wrote the input, judged the result and fixed it, which is authorship in the ordinary sense. On the other, the output came from patterns in work that other people made and did not agree to, and typing a sentence is not the same act as writing a thousand lines. I am not going to resolve it and neither is your grade.

## Slide 8: You are graded on the argument
- State your position clearly
- Give the strongest case for it
- Give the strongest case against it
- Say what would change your mind
Speaker notes: There is no correct side today, so here is what I am actually grading. Four parts. State where you land. Argue it. Then argue against yourself, honestly, which is the part everybody skips and the part worth the most. And name what specifically would change your mind. If nothing would change your mind, you do not have a position, you have a preference.
