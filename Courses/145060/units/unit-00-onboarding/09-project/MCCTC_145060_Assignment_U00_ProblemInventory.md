# Problem Inventory
## 145060 Programming · Unit 0 · Week 1 · Due Friday, September 11

**Note on artifact type.** This is a written deliverable, not a coding project, so it
has no reference implementation. It is filed here because it is the backlog every
project in this course and in your senior capstone gets drawn from.

---

## Why you are doing this

Every project you build for the next two years has to come from somewhere. The two
ways students usually pick a project both go badly.

The first is picking something impressive. You choose a project because it sounds
good when described out loud, discover in week three that you have no idea what it
should actually do, and finish something that works and that nobody wanted.

The second is picking nothing until the deadline forces it. Then you build whatever
is small enough to finish, which is almost never the thing you would have been proud
of.

The way out is having a list already. Not ideas, and specifically not app ideas.
**Problems.** Real ones, that real people you can name actually have.

This list is not busywork and it is not a warmup. In your senior year you will need
a capstone project with a stakeholder who wants the outcome. Students who have that
list on day one build something real. Students who do not spend three weeks deciding.

---

## What you will produce

A file called `problem-inventory.md` in your repository, containing **ten entries.**

Each entry is three lines:

```
PROBLEM: <what is annoying, repetitive, or broken>
WHO:     <the specific person or group who has it>
HOW OFTEN: <how many times, in what period of time>
```

That is the whole format. Ten of those.

---

## What counts and what does not

**This counts:**

> PROBLEM: The team roster on the board is out of date, so people show up for shifts
> that were reassigned two weeks ago.
> WHO: The eleven people who work weekend shifts at the shop, and the manager who
> gets the phone calls.
> HOW OFTEN: Someone shows up wrong about twice a month.

**This does not count:**

> PROBLEM: There should be an app that helps students study better.

Three things are wrong with it. It names no specific annoyance, no specific person,
and no frequency. It is also a description of an app rather than a problem, which is
the failure this assignment exists to prevent.

### The four rules

1. **Name the annoyance, not the solution.** "I have to text four people the same
   information every week" is a problem. "An app that texts people" is a solution
   you picked before you understood the problem.

2. **Name a specific person or group.** "Students" is not specific. "The six people
   in my third period group project" is. "My mom" is. If you cannot name who, you
   probably invented it.

3. **State how often it happens.** Twice a month, every Sunday night, three times a
   practice. Frequency is what separates a real annoyance from a thing you noticed
   once.

4. **Do not describe an app that already exists.** If your entry is a slightly worse
   version of something on your phone, cross it out. You are allowed to build things
   that exist, and you are not allowed to pretend you found the problem yourself.

### Where to look, if you are stuck

At least three of your ten have to be problems **you personally have had in the last
month.** Start there. These prompts work:

- What was annoying about getting here this morning?
- What do you have to do more than once a week that you dislike?
- What do your parents, a coach, or a boss ask you for over and over?
- What is the last thing you could not find when you needed it?
- What does somebody in your family complain about constantly?
- What is something at your job that everybody agrees is stupid and nobody fixes?
- What information do you keep in your head that you wish was written down somewhere?
- What do you have to explain to people more than once?

The last two are the richest. Most software worth building replaces something a
person is currently doing from memory.

## Requirements

- [ ] Ten entries, each with all three lines filled in
- [ ] At least three are problems you personally had in the last month
- [ ] No entry is a restatement of an app that already exists
- [ ] Every WHO names a specific person or a specific group, not a category
- [ ] Every HOW OFTEN contains a number and a time period
- [ ] The file is named `problem-inventory.md` and is in your repository
- [ ] It is committed with a message explaining why the file exists
- [ ] It is pushed

**No personal information.** Do not put anyone's full name, phone number, address,
schedule, or medical or financial details in this file. It is going to a public
repository and it stays there permanently. Write "my manager" and not their name.
This is the same rule as everything else in this course, and this assignment is the
first place it actually bites.

## Grading

This is scored under **Written & Documentation**, which is 20 percent of your grade.
30 points.

| Points | Standard |
|---|---|
| 27-30 | Ten entries, all three lines real and specific. At least three are clearly first-hand. Several name a frequency you could only know by having lived it. |
| 21-26 | Ten entries. Two or three have a vague WHO or a missing frequency. Most are genuine. |
| 15-20 | Ten entries exist, but several are app descriptions rather than problems, or WHO is a category like "students" throughout. |
| 8-14 | Fewer than ten entries, or the format is not followed. |
| 0-7 | Not submitted, or not in the repository. Work that is not in a repository is not submitted. |

**The thing that moves a 22 to a 28** is almost never more entries. It is going back
through the ten you have and making the WHO line specific. That takes about six
minutes and it is the difference between a list you will use and a list you will
never open again.

## What happens to this list

You keep it. You add to it. It is not collected once and forgotten.

- **Unit 1** you build a command-line tool that solves one item from this list.
- **Unit 2** your problem drop connects to it.
- **Units 3 through 7** your text adventure is separate, and this list keeps growing
  in the background.
- **Senior year** your capstone comes from here, and the entries that survived two
  years of you looking at them are the ones worth building.

Every time something annoys you between now and graduation, add it. The list gets
more valuable the longer it runs, and the entries you write when you are actually
annoyed are better than the ones you write in class on a Friday.
