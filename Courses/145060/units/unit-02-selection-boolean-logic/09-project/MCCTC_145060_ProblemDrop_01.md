## How a problem drop works

Nobody is going to tell you what to build. You get a real situation from a real kind of
person, written the way people actually describe their problems: out of order, with some
opinions mixed in, and with the thing they ask for not quite matching the thing that hurts.

You have 40 minutes. You cannot solve all of it. **Deciding what to solve is part of the
grade.**

Your instructor will stop by in the last 15 minutes and ask you one question about a choice
you made. It takes about a minute.

## The situation

This is a message Dana left for a friend who "knows computers." Dana runs a small family
pizza place. Dana and the shop are a composite, invented for this drop.

> Okay, so, Friday nights are killing us.
>
> The phones do not stop. We have two lines and both are ringing from 5 until close, and
> people sit on hold and hang up, and that is lost orders. I have thought about hiring
> somebody to do nothing but answer phones, but I cannot really afford that.
>
> Half the calls are not even orders. They are people asking if we deliver to them. And
> that should take ten seconds, but it turns into this whole conversation. My brother says
> yes to everybody. My daughter says no to anybody past the water tower, which is about 4
> miles out. So a customer calls back the next week and says "you delivered here last
> Friday," and they are right, and now we are arguing on the phone while the other line
> rings.
>
> And the drivers are mad. Last Friday somebody sent Marco 6 miles out for a $9 order. That
> is twenty minutes there and back for nothing. Past the water tower I lose money on anything
> under about $25. A driver can get out about 6 miles and back in twenty minutes, and past
> that the next orders go cold.
>
> We stop taking delivery orders at 10:30 because we close at 11. Somebody always calls at
> 10:40 and gets mad about that too. Pickup is always fine, usually ready in 20 minutes, and
> delivery usually takes 35 to 45 minutes.
>
> Can you make something that answers the phone? Or I do not know. Something. I need
> Friday to stop being a fight.

## What you hand in

A new folder in your repository, `problem-drop-01/`, containing:

### 1. `DROP.md`, with these four headings

- **The real problem.** In two or three sentences. Not what Dana asked for. What is actually
  causing the pain.
- **Who uses what I built, and when.** One or two sentences.
- **What my program does.** One or two sentences.
- **What I chose not to do, and why.** At least two things.

### 2. A working program

Whatever you decided to build, using only what you know: `input`, conversions, arithmetic,
strings, `if`/`elif`/`else`, `and`/`or`/`not`, and `match`. It must run with no traceback on
at least three inputs you choose, and you must paste those three runs at the bottom of
`DROP.md`.

### 3. A commit

Committed and pushed before the block ends.

---

## How to spend 40 minutes

| Minutes | Do this |
|---|---|
| 0-8 | Read the message twice. Write **The real problem** in `DROP.md` before you open a Python file. |
| 8-33 | Build the smallest thing that fixes the real problem. Run it at the boundaries. |
| 33-38 | Paste three runs. Write the other three headings. |
| 38-40 | Commit and push. |

**If you are still writing code at minute 34, stop.** A program that handles less, with an
honest `DROP.md`, scores higher than a bigger program with no explanation.
